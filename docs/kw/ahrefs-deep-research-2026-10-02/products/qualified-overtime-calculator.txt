  - heading "Choose the method that fits your records" [level=2]
  - paragraph: Use a separately reported qualified amount if you have one. Otherwise, choose total overtime pay or enter your regular rate and hours. Keep the statement you used so payroll or your tax preparer can check the result.
  - article:
    - heading "Separate FLSA premium" [level=3]
    - paragraph: If a statement separately reports the FLSA overtime premium, use that amount as the starting estimate.
  - article:
    - heading "Total time-and-a-half overtime" [level=3]
    - paragraph: For standard time-and-a-half pay, the premium is one third of total overtime earnings. The rest is the regular pay for those hours.
  - article:
    - heading "Higher overtime rate" [level=3]
    - paragraph: If overtime was paid above time-and-a-half, the estimate should isolate only the FLSA-required half-time premium portion.
  - article:
    - heading "No separate statement" [level=3]
    - paragraph: If no annual statement is available, estimate from regular rate and overtime hours, then keep notes showing why that method was used.
  - heading "How to read the result" [level=2]
  - paragraph: The result estimates your qualified overtime compensation. Your deduction may be lower because of annual caps, income limits, or other tax rules.
  - paragraph: For an annual total, you can also review the filing-status cap and check whether your MAGI may trigger a phase-out warning.
