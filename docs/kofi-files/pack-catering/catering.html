How to work this list

Drop-off is a volume game

No staff, no rentals, thin margins, fast turns — it fills calendars between full-service bookings. Don't price it like staffed service.

Buffet math

Target 30% food cost: price = ingredient cost × 3.3. If the menu costs more than that, cut SKUs before you cut margin.

Staffing prices separately

Servers add $25–40 per person in real cost. Never bake it in — itemize it, or every quote looks high.

The bar is the profit center

Beverage service margins run 60–70%. Lead with it on every quote.

Contract anchors

50% deposit at booking, final headcount 7 days out, balance due day-of. Below ~50 guests, sell drop-off or refer out.

How to judge YOUR local market — the five-step method

  1. Start from the national band — pages 1–2 of this kit.
  2. Apply the regional adjustment — coastal metros +20–50% across our research; small towns slightly under. Your state's cost-of-living index is a reasonable proxy.
  3. Compute your cost floor — (your loaded hours + materials/supplies) for the job. No quote below the floor, ever. It's the line between a business and a hobby.
  4. Validate locally — three published local prices (Angi/HomeGuide zip-level or competitor pages) tell you where the market actually clears.
  5. Set your band and review quarterly — booked out two weeks straight? Move up. Empty for a month? Your floor is above your market.

Worked example — Buffet dinner for 80 guests, corporate event, Atlanta GA

Step 1. National: buffet $27 avg; basic buffets $35–75 → pick $48/pp mid-band.

Step 2. Food cost: $16/pp × 80 = $1,280 ingredients at 33% food cost.

Step 3. Staffing: 4 servers × 6 hrs × $25 = $600; rentals $400.

Step 4. Price: $48 × 80 = $3,840 + staffing/rentals itemized → ~35% margin after all-in. Bar add-on at $30/pp is your 60%+ margin sweetener.

Catering prices are per-person, but the margin is per-line-item. Itemize everything.

Strategy — the five plays that move the number

The service-style ladder

Drop-off → buffet → plated → full-service. Each rung adds staffing and rentals — price each rung separately and let clients climb.

Buffet math

30% food cost target: menu price = ingredient cost × 3.3. When the menu drifts expensive, cut SKUs — never cut the multiplier.

Staffing itemized, always

$25–40/pp of real cost baked into a per-person price makes every quote look high. Line it, explain it, own it.

The bar is the profit center

Beverage service runs 60–70% margin. Lead every quote with it; it's the difference between a job and a business.

Contract anchors

50% deposit at booking, final headcount 7 days out, balance due day-of. Below ~50 guests, sell drop-off or refer out — plated service for 30 loses money at any price.
