How to work this list

Bundles beat hourly

An hourly quote punishes you for being fast. A bundle folds your drive time, trip cost and cleanup into one honest number — clients compare bundles, not clocks.

The bundle math

(hours × your target rate) + materials markup + trip fee, per job. If a bundle loses money on paper, split it into two jobs or drop it.

Minimum visit charge

Never take a job below your smallest bundle + trip fee. 'Can you just look at it' calls get the free 10-minute walkthrough and a written quote — not a price shouted over the phone.

Materials markup

Bill materials at cost + 10–15%, stated on the quote. The surprise on the invoice is what kills repeat work.

When to raise

Booked two weeks out for a month straight? Raise your top two bundles ~10%. Demand is telling you the number was low.

How to judge YOUR local market — the five-step method

  1. Start from the national band — pages 1–2 of this kit.
  2. Apply the regional adjustment — coastal metros +20–50% across our research; small towns slightly under. Your state's cost-of-living index is a reasonable proxy.
  3. Compute your cost floor — (your loaded hours + materials/supplies) for the job. No quote below the floor, ever. It's the line between a business and a hobby.
  4. Validate locally — three published local prices (Angi/HomeGuide zip-level or competitor pages) tell you where the market actually clears.
  5. Set your band and review quarterly — booked out two weeks straight? Move up. Empty for a month? Your floor is above your market.

Worked example — TV mount + faucet replacement, same visit, Phoenix AZ

Step 1. Bundles: TV $100 + faucet $135 = $235 booked together.

Step 2. Trip economics: one truck roll instead of two saves you ~$25 in drive cost — offer the second job at −10% ($122) and you still clear more than two trips.

Step 3. Cost floor: 3 hrs × $45 loaded + $25 materials/trip = $160. $235 → 32% margin before materials markup.

Step 4. Validate: Thumbtack local TV-mount rates $85–150; your $100 with bracket markup sits mid-market.

The second job in the same visit is nearly free margin. Sell the same-day bundle.

Strategy — the five plays that move the number

Same-day pairing

Two bundles in one visit halve the trip cost per job — offer ~10% off the second bundle and you still beat two separate trips.

The trip fee is real

Out-of-area jobs carry a $40–75 trip fee (Jobber; LawnSite pattern). Bundle neighbors onto one day and the fee becomes margin instead of friction.

Materials transparency

Cost + 10–15% on the written quote, receipts stapled to the invoice. Transparency is what gets you called for the next bundle.

Raise through scope, not rate

Add 'furniture moved', 'haul-away old part', 'smart-device setup' as $25–50 line items before touching your base rates.

Reviews compound

Ask for the review at the door with the payment link still open. Each 5-star review raises your close rate on the next 'how much?' call.
