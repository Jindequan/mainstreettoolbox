How to work this list

First cleans are deep cleans

Quote the first visit 40–50% over standard — years of build-up live in baseboards, vents and appliance interiors. Maintenance pricing starts on visit two.

The recurring discount buys density

15–20% off for weekly, ~10% for biweekly. You're not giving money away — you're eliminating the acquisition cost of every future visit.

Bathrooms price higher than bedrooms

Scrubbing a bathroom is the slowest work in the house. Per-bathroom charges should out-weigh per-bedroom ones.

The hourly floor

Your floor = (target wage + supplies) ÷ your solo speed. The band tells you if you're in market; the floor tells you if you're profitable.

Market position

Coastal metros run 30–50% above these bands; small towns a little under.

How to judge YOUR local market — the five-step method

  1. Start from the national band — pages 1–2 of this kit.
  2. Apply the regional adjustment — coastal metros +20–50% across our research; small towns slightly under. Your state's cost-of-living index is a reasonable proxy.
  3. Compute your cost floor — (your loaded hours + materials/supplies) for the job. No quote below the floor, ever. It's the line between a business and a hobby.
  4. Validate locally — three published local prices (Angi/HomeGuide zip-level or competitor pages) tell you where the market actually clears.
  5. Set your band and review quarterly — booked out two weeks straight? Move up. Empty for a month? Your floor is above your market.

Worked example — Deep clean, 3 bed / 2 bath house, Seattle WA

Step 1. National band: deep clean $200–450.

Step 2. Regional: Seattle is a coastal metro running +30–50% over national → plausible local band $260–675.

Step 3. Cost floor: 2 cleaners × 6 hrs × $45 loaded = $540. Never quote below your floor.

Step 4. Validate: check 3 local companies' published rates, land at $550–650, and quote $625.

The national band is where you START. The city you stand in decides where you finish.

Strategy — the five plays that move the number

Tier the menu, always

Three levels: maintenance / deep / move-out. Most clients pick the middle — build the one you want to sell as the middle option and let the other two frame it.

Discount the relationship, not the job

Weekly −15–20%, biweekly −10%. Every discount is tied to a recurring commitment, never to a one-time negotiation. A one-off discount trains the client to push.

Raise through the menu

New menu twice a year, two weeks' notice, under 10% a year for regulars. Raise the underpriced lines the most — bathroom-heavy clients first.

The first-visit rule

First cleans are deep cleans at deep prices. Discounting the first clean attracts the worst clients and anchors you low forever.
