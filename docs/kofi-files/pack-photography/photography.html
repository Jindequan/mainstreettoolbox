How to work this list

Packages beat hourly for weddings

Clients fear open-ended bills. Quote full-day coverage as a package; keep the hourly rate as your internal math, not the headline.

The three-tier trick

Good / Better / Best. Most buyers pick the middle — build your target package as the middle option and price the others around it.

Second shooter economics

A second shooter costs you $150–300 and prices at +$500–800 on the package. It's the highest-margin line on the menu.

Albums and prints are where margin hides

Price delivery separately from coverage. Clients who balk at $4,500 still add a $600 album.

The booking fee

20–30% non-refundable booking fee secures the date. It filters tyre-kickers and pays for the hold.

Rush delivery is a price lever

Standard 6-week gallery delivery; 2-week rush = +25%. Same files, priced impatience.

How to judge YOUR local market — the five-step method

  1. Start from the national band — pages 1–2 of this kit.
  2. Apply the regional adjustment — coastal metros +20–50% across our research; small towns slightly under. Your state's cost-of-living index is a reasonable proxy.
  3. Compute your cost floor — (your loaded hours + materials/supplies) for the job. No quote below the floor, ever. It's the line between a business and a hobby.
  4. Validate locally — three published local prices (Angi/HomeGuide zip-level or competitor pages) tell you where the market actually clears.
  5. Set your band and review quarterly — booked out two weeks straight? Move up. Empty for a month? Your floor is above your market.

Worked example — 6-hour wedding, mid-size city (~500k population)

Step 1. National: avg $3,000; package band $2,000–5,000.

Step 2. Regional: mid-size city ≈ national, not coastal → target $2,800–3,400 for 6-hr coverage.

Step 3. Cost floor: 10 hrs (shoot+edit) × $50 + $300 second-shooter share + $150 gear amortization ≈ $950 → healthy margin at $3,000.

Step 4. Validate: 3 local photographers' published packages; position yours $100 above the cheapest with a bigger gallery promise.

Strategy — the five plays that move the number

Three tiers, middle wins

Good/Better/Best wedding packages with the middle as your target. The top tier exists to make the middle look reasonable.

Charge for the promise, not the hours

Clients buy the gallery, the culling, the edit, the album design. Hours are your math; the deliverable is the price.

The booking fee filters clients

20–30% non-refundable booking fee. Real clients pay it; tyre-kickers taught you its value.

Raise after every sold-out season

When your calendar fills, next season's prices go up 10–15%. Scarcity is the market telling you to move.

Albums are a second sale

Price coverage clean, then sell albums and prints post-wedding when the emotional peak does the selling.
