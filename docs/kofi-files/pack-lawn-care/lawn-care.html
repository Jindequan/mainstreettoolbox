How to work this list

Price the stop, not the grass

Mowing is route economics: 12 lawns on one street at $45 each beats 4 lawns across town at $70. Density beats rate.

Biweekly costs more per visit

Longer grass takes longer to cut — +15–25% per visit (Housecall Pro). Say it out loud when clients ask to switch.

Leaf cleanup: charge the job, mind the weather

Wet leaves can double the time. Quote dry-season rates and add a weather clause for late-fall bookings.

The stop fee

Out-of-route jobs carry a $40–135 stop fee (LawnSite operator reports). Bundle neighbors onto one route day and the fee becomes margin.

Pre-pay seasonal

5–10% off for the season paid upfront — their cash, your guaranteed calendar.

How to judge YOUR local market — the five-step method

  1. Start from the national band — pages 1–2 of this kit.
  2. Apply the regional adjustment — coastal metros +20–50% across our research; small towns slightly under. Your state's cost-of-living index is a reasonable proxy.
  3. Compute your cost floor — (your loaded hours + materials/supplies) for the job. No quote below the floor, ever. It's the line between a business and a hobby.
  4. Validate locally — three published local prices (Angi/HomeGuide zip-level or competitor pages) tell you where the market actually clears.
  5. Set your band and review quarterly — booked out two weeks straight? Move up. Empty for a month? Your floor is above your market.

Worked example — ¼-acre weekly mowing, Dallas TX

Step 1. National band: $40–60 weekly for a standard yard; Texas cost base sits near national.

Step 2. Route math: dense subdivisions → 12 lawns per day at $45–55 each beats scattered $70 jobs.

Step 3. Cost floor: 30 min on site + 15 min drive+dump = $30 loaded cost per stop at $40/hr equipment-in. $45+ keeps >40% margin.

Step 4. Validate: check GreenPal's zip-level data and two local crew prices; land at $45–50 and hold it.

Mowing is a route business. The lawn is the unit; the ROUTE is the business.

Strategy — the five plays that move the number

Sell the route, price the stop

Every lawn you add on an existing street costs you minutes, not a truck roll. Cluster pricing: full price solo, −$5 when two neighbors book the same day.

Biweekly is a different job

Longer grass takes longer — +15–25% per visit. Quote it as its own service, not a discount on weekly.

Seasonal cash

Pre-paid seasonal mowing (−10%) and fall cleanup bundles front-load cash for equipment. Aeration/overseeding in fall is the highest-margin add-on you sell.

Minimums protect you

One-off mows below $40 don't pay for the truck roll. Minimum visit charge, stated on the phone.

Raise through capacity

When you're turning work away, raise new-client rates 10% first — regulars follow on the next season renewal.
