How to work this list

Price size honestly

A large dog takes roughly twice the table time of a small one — the $55/$100 split is honest, and honesty is what size-based pricing communicates.

De-shedding is the spring & fall upsell

$30 on top of nearly every bath during shedding season. Offer it before you start, not after.

Flea treatment is a protocol, not a groom

Quote it separately — sanitation requirements make it a different job, and bundling it reads as a surcharge.

The recurring plan is the business

Every 6–8 weeks. Book the next visit before the client leaves the shop — the discount you offer is cheaper than the re-acquisition.

Handling fees for difficult dogs

Aggressive or severe-mat cases: charge a handling fee or refer out. One bite costs more than the groom's margin.

How to judge YOUR local market — the five-step method

  1. Start from the national band — pages 1–2 of this kit.
  2. Apply the regional adjustment — coastal metros +20–50% across our research; small towns slightly under. Your state's cost-of-living index is a reasonable proxy.
  3. Compute your cost floor — (your loaded hours + materials/supplies) for the job. No quote below the floor, ever. It's the line between a business and a hobby.
  4. Validate locally — three published local prices (Angi/HomeGuide zip-level or competitor pages) tell you where the market actually clears.
  5. Set your band and review quarterly — booked out two weeks straight? Move up. Empty for a month? Your floor is above your market.

Worked example — Full groom, 60 lb golden retriever, Seattle WA

Step 1. National band: large dog $70–150.

Step 2. Regional: Seattle sits at the upper band → $90–130.

Step 3. Cost floor: 2 hrs groomer × $30 loaded + $8 supplies + $6 share of rent = $74. $95+ keeps margin.

Step 4. Validate: 3 Seattle mobile/shop prices; publish $105 with de-shedding at $35.

Big dog, big city — both push you up the band, and both are honest reasons.

Strategy — the five plays that move the number

Price by size tiers, publish them

$45/$55/$100 size-based tiers filter the 'how much for a golden?' call and set expectations before the table.

Seasonal upsells carry the year

De-shedding in spring/fall, flea & tick in summer — offer before the groom, add as a line, teach the front desk to ask.

The 6–8 week plan is the business

Pre-book the next visit at checkout with a small loyalty discount. A full rebooking book is a sellable asset.

Handling fees protect you

Severe mats or behavior cases: +$20–40 handling fee or refer out. One bite costs more than a month of grooms.

Raise with notice

Post the new menu a month out; grandfather the senior dogs' bath price. Loyalty follows fairness.
