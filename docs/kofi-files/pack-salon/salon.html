How to work this list

Price your chair, not the market

Your real cost per bookable hour = (booth rent or commission target) ÷ booked hours. Services below that number are charity, and charity doesn't buy product.

Color is the margin engine

Highlights take 2–3× a cut's time — price them honestly at 2–3×. A $200 full highlight is a $70 cut with more chair time, not a luxury.

Raise with a new menu

Announce two weeks ahead, raise the underpriced services most, keep the increase under 10%/year for regulars.

Commission negotiation

New stylists keep 40–50%, moving toward 60% with a full book — that's the standard ladder. Know your number before the lease conversation.

Retail adds quiet revenue

50–100% margin on retail products adds 5–10% to revenue with zero chair time.

How to judge YOUR local market — the five-step method

  1. Start from the national band — pages 1–2 of this kit.
  2. Apply the regional adjustment — coastal metros +20–50% across our research; small towns slightly under. Your state's cost-of-living index is a reasonable proxy.
  3. Compute your cost floor — (your loaded hours + materials/supplies) for the job. No quote below the floor, ever. It's the line between a business and a hobby.
  4. Validate locally — three published local prices (Angi/HomeGuide zip-level or competitor pages) tell you where the market actually clears.
  5. Set your band and review quarterly — booked out two weeks straight? Move up. Empty for a month? Your floor is above your market.

Worked example — Setting your chair rate, independent booth-rent stylist, Denver

Step 1. Target income: $60,000/year → $5,000/mo → $1,250/wk.

Step 2. Capacity: 30 bookable hours × 6 hrs productive/day ≈ 25 services/wk at your mix.

Step 3. Cost floor: rent $400/wk + supplies 12% → your average service must gross ≥ $70 to clear $55 net.

Step 4. Menu: cut $65, color $140, highlights $200 — weighted mix averages $85/service → $2,125/wk gross, ~$1,575 after rent and supplies. Raise color when booked out 2 weeks.

Your chair is a business with one employee. The menu is just the math made visible.

Strategy — the five plays that move the number

Price the chair-hour first

Every service price must clear your chair-hour cost plus margin. Services below it exist only to fill gaps between color appointments.

Color is the margin engine

Highlights take 2–3× a cut's time — price them at 2–3×. A $200 full highlight is a $70 cut with more chair time, not a luxury.

Raise with a new menu

Two weeks' notice, under 10%/year for regulars, biggest moves on the underpriced lines. A new printed menu makes it policy, not a negotiation.

Retail is the quiet raise

50–100% margin on take-home product adds 5–10% revenue with zero extra chair time.

Know your commission ladder

40–50% keep as a new stylist, 60% with a full book — the standard ladder. Your book is your leverage; track it.
